# Design: "Am I on track?" check-in on a rate-card model (no session-level data)

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-11-03 (revised the same day for the rate-card / no-session-data direction)
Branch: front-door
Repo: PaulHoover32/therapay-app
Status: APPROVED
Mode: Startup

## Problem Statement
A 1099 therapist on a platform like Grow, Headway or Alma can't tell, during the year, whether she's on pace to match last year's income, or to grow it by the 5–10% she wants. She "just sort of hopes it will happen." Therapists on several platforms have it worse.

Therapay is a very cheap AI financial advisor that answers **"Am I on track, and what would change that?"** inside Claude or ChatGPT. It does this **without collecting session-level data.** Per-session records (a date, a CPT code and a payer for each appointment) are close to a clinical record and create HIPAA exposure, and with only Stripe payout data they can't be reconstructed honestly anyway.

So the model is:
- **Stripe payouts** are the truth for cash received.
- **A per-payer rate card plus a monthly count of sessions per payer** gives expected earnings and the payer analysis.

Later it benchmarks her against verified peer earnings by platform, payer and state.

## Demand Evidence
- **One real user with real pain: the founder's wife**, a 1099 therapist on one platform (Grow, paid through Stripe Express). In Paul's words: "by the end of the year, she really doesn't know if she's going to be on track with what she did the prior year... no confidence in her ability to grow by like 5-10% every year."
- **No behavioral evidence yet.** She hasn't used Therapay with her own data. Nobody outside the household has been asked.
- **Category signal (unverified, from web research):** Heard charges about $149–199/month for bookkeeping plus taxes for private practice. Willingness to pay for pacing specifically is unproven.

## Status Quo
- Today she looks at her platform's earnings view and hopes. Founder: "I'm guessing."
- Platforms show their own earnings only. None shows pace against a goal, a same-period comparison with last year, or a view across platforms.
- Related pain, also unverified and from web research:
  - insurer rate changes
  - billing-accuracy complaints
  - deposit memos that don't match platform names
  - December sessions that land in the next tax year

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
- **User zero:** the founder's wife, on Grow, paid through Stripe Express.
- **Next users:** therapists in her network, especially anyone on two or more platforms. None are identified yet.
- **Narrowest wedge:** "How am I tracking?" in Claude returns:
  - cash received year to date vs the same months last year
  - a projected year-end figure vs her goal
  - the gap, turned into an action (for example "about 5 more sessions a month at your average rate", or "your Aetna rate is your lowest")

  No peer comparison yet.

## Constraints
- **No session-level data.** There are no per-appointment rows, no session dates and no per-session CPT codes. The finest grain stored is **payer × month counts**, plus a rate per payer (and optionally per service type). Client names are never stored, and nothing takes free text.
- **Small-group guardrail.** Monthly buckets, and no display that could single out a client. For example, "1 Kaiser client in March" is never shown on its own in shared or peer views.
- **No portal automation in production.** Therapay never logs into a platform portal on a therapist's behalf. Data arrives as files or exports she provides.
- **HIPAA status is undecided.** Attorney review is required before onboarding therapists beyond user zero (see Dependencies).
- **One developer, on the live stack:** Vercel, Supabase with RLS, OAuth via Supabase.
- **"Very cheap"** relative to bookkeeping services.
- **The web app is only the front door:** login, account management and connect setup (`/login`, `/account`, `/connect`, OAuth consent).
  - It gets **no product functionality**: no dashboards, no rate-card forms, no charts, no in-app chat.
  - Everything that drives Therapay runs in Claude or ChatGPT, through MCP tools, MCP prompts and Skills.
  - The old web app (`/dashboard`, `/planner`, `/profile`, the in-app chat at `/api/chat`) is frozen. It isn't extended, and its removal is a separate cleanup.

## Premises
Agreed in session (v3, after two corrections from the founder):
1. **The job is pacing, not tracking.** Data collection exists only to answer "Am I on track to match last year and grow 5–10%, and what would change that?"
2. **No session-level data.**
   - Cash comes from Stripe or other payout exports.
   - Expected earnings come from the rate card × monthly volume per payer.
   - No-shows and late cancels are monthly counts too, including the $0 ones.
3. **His wife is user zero and goes first.** She uses it with her real data, unprompted, for 2–3 weeks before any further build.
4. **Claude or ChatGPT is the product.** The pacing answer, rate-card setup, monthly check-in and imports all happen in chat. The web app only signs people in and connects them.
5. **The peer cohort is the long-term edge, not the launch.** It works best on clean rate cards and needs density plus a privacy floor of about 20 peers per bucket.
6. **Cheap is the position.**
7. **Five more real therapists within 30 days**, and attorney sign-off on the HIPAA posture before they're onboarded.

Rejected along the way:
- v1 premise #2: "tracking is too weak a wedge". It misread the product.
- v2: per-session logging as the input to pacing. It conflicts with the no-session-data model.

## Recommended Approach
**Approach A: rate-card check-in.**

### Data model (one migration)
- **`payer_rates`**
  - Columns: `therapist_id`, `payer` (validated against reference payers and platforms), `platform` (nullable), `service` (one of `individual_long` / `individual_short` / `intake` / `couples` / `group`, or `any`), `rate` (numeric), `effective_from` (month).
  - A new rate gets a new row with a later `effective_from`, so history is kept when Aetna cuts a rate.
- **`monthly_volume`**
  - Columns: `therapist_id`, `month` (first of month), `payer`, `platform` (nullable), `completed` (integer), `no_shows` (integer), `late_cancels` (integer), `late_cancel_fee` (numeric, default 0), `source` (`manual` / `bulk_import`).
  - Unique on `(therapist_id, month, payer, platform)`. Entering a month again updates it; it doesn't duplicate.
- **`payouts` changes**
  - Unique on `(therapist_id, external_id)` where `external_id` isn't null. This fixes the current bug where re-importing the same Stripe `po_` ID creates duplicates. Rows without an ID are still never blocked; they get the existing overlap warning.
  - Add `arrival_date` (money in the bank) beside `payout_date` (platform paid out). Use `payout_date` for attribution and `arrival_date` for cash-in-bank views.
- **`sessions`** stops being written by MCP. Existing rows, which are test or seed data only, stay for the old web dashboard, which is frozen. A later cleanup can drop the table once the dashboard is retired.
- RLS on every new table traces to `auth.uid()` through `therapists.user_id`, the same as `payouts`.

### MCP prompts and server instructions (the "Skills" layer)
- **MCP prompts** (`registerPrompt`) for the three recurring workflows. In Claude they show up as slash-command-style shortcuts that run a guided flow with the tools:
  - **"Set up Therapay":** profile, then rate card, then import last year's payouts, then first `check_in`.
  - **"Monthly check-in":** last month's counts per payer (`log_month`), import new payouts, then `check_in`.
  - **"Am I on track?":** runs `check_in` and explains the levers in plain language.
- **Server `instructions`** carry the guidance that used to live in web pages:
  - how to explain data-source choices (Stripe vs portal vs bank statement)
  - the no-client-details rule
  - always ask for counts, never dates or clients
- **A Claude Skill** (a `SKILL.md` package users can add to Claude) is the next step if prompts prove too limited in Claude, or for users who want the guidance offline. Not in this build.
- **ChatGPT:** support for MCP prompts is uneven, so the server instructions and tool descriptions alone have to be enough to run every flow. That's tested with a ChatGPT developer-mode connector.

### MCP tools
- **Retire** `log_session` and `bulk_log_sessions`. They're removed from `registerTherapayTools`, so Claude can't collect per-appointment data anymore. `list_sessions` goes too.
- **`set_rate_card`:** sets rates per payer, optionally per service type. The description tells Claude to ask "what does each payer pay you per session?" and to accept approximate numbers.
- **`log_month`:** takes a month plus per-payer `completed`, `no_shows`, `late_cancels` and optional `late_cancel_fee`. It upserts. The description tells Claude to ask it as a quick monthly check-in, in counts only, with no dates or clients.
- **`import_payouts`** stays. It deduplicates by external ID, and the preview says "N already imported, skipped". It warns when an export looks cut short, for example when it ends months before today.
- **`check_in`** and **`set_goal`** are specified below. `list_payouts`, `get_reconciliation`, `undo_import` and `list_imports` stay. Reconciliation's "expected" switches from sessions to rate × volume.

### Income series (single input to everything in `check_in`)
- Monthly cells, keyed by payer × month.
- **Received:** paid payouts attributed to a month by `workDate()`, as today.
- **Expected:** `Σ rate(payer, service, month) × completed + late_cancels × late_cancel_fee`, from `payer_rates` and `monthly_volume`. When she enters only one blended rate per payer (service `any`), that rate is used.
- **Best available per cell:** received if any payouts exist for that cell, else expected. This is the same rule as reconciliation's `totals.income`, in a new `lib/pacing.ts` built on `loadReconciliation`.
- **Basis flag:** `basis: { this_year, last_year }`, each `payouts`, `rate_card` or `mixed`. When the two years differ, it adds a data note.

### Settled cutoff and expected payouts
- **Settled month:** a month is settled once `month_end + L < today`. L is the largest typical payout lag among payers with income in the last 12 months, falling back to 14 days.
- **Typical lags:** about 18 days for insurance and 7 for cash, and they stay configurable per payer and platform. The cash default changes from 2 to 7 in this release.
- **Unsettled months** count as `in_progress`, valued at **expected minus received so far**, and labeled "estimated, still arriving".
- **Next payouts forecast:** a list of the next 1 or 2 expected payout amounts and dates, from expected volume and typical timing. In-progress estimates never count toward YTD, pace or projection.

### Windows (always "today"; goal year = current calendar year)
- **YTD:** January through the last settled month, vs the same months last year.
- **Pace:** the last 3 settled months (or the n available, labeled `pace_months`) vs the same n months last year.
- **Projection, when last year has income in every month:** YTD ÷ (last year's same-months share of the full year). If that share is 0, the projection is omitted.
- **Projection, otherwise:** with 3 or more settled months, YTD + pace × remaining months, flagged "no seasonality data". With fewer, it's omitted.

### Edge cases
- **No goal:**
  - The target is last year's total (`target_source: "last_year"`), and the description offers `set_goal`.
  - If last year is incomplete, the response omits the gap and levers.
- **Zero settled months** (until Jan 31 + L, roughly the first 5–9 weeks of the year): only `in_progress`, the next-payouts forecast and last year's total are shown.
- **No rate card yet:** `check_in` still works from payouts alone. The session-count lever is omitted, and the response suggests `set_rate_card`.
- **No payouts yet:** `check_in` works from the rate card and volume, flagged `basis: rate_card`, and suggests importing payouts for accuracy.

### Levers (available only when there's a target and at least one settled month)
- **Dollars per month needed:** (target − YTD) ÷ months remaining after the settled cutoff. In-progress estimates aren't subtracted.
- **Sessions per month needed:** dollars per month needed ÷ her blended rate (volume-weighted average rate over the last 3 months, from the rate card). Shown only with a rate card plus at least one month of volume.
- **Payer mix:** her highest- and lowest-paying payers by rate, plus what moving 2 sessions a month from the lowest to the highest would add over the rest of the year. Shown only with two or more payers on the rate card.
- **No-show cost:** last 3 months' no-shows and $0 late cancels × blended rate, meaning "money left on the table". Shown when those counts exist.
- **Weeks off she can afford:** shown only when she's ahead of her goal. max(0, (projected − target) ÷ (pace ÷ 4.33)).

### `set_goal` (numbers only)
- **Inputs:** exactly one of `target_income` or `growth_pct_over_last_year`.
  - Growth requires last year to have income in every month. Otherwise it errors and asks for a dollar target.
  - Neither or both is an error.
- **Optional `basis`:** `received` (default) or `earned`. It decides whether progress is measured from payouts or from rate × volume.
- **Write path:**
  - A new `lib/goals.ts` `setActiveGoal()` deactivates this year's active goals and inserts one `goals` row with `last_modified_by='ai'`.
  - It writes no `recommendations` row, so no free text is stored.
  - It's a no-op when the active goal already has the same target and basis, so `idempotentHint: true` is accurate. `destructiveHint: true`.
- **Required columns:**
  - `target_avg_payout`: her blended rate, or the prior goal's value, or 0.
  - `target_weekly_hours`: (target ÷ blended rate ÷ 48 weeks) × her profile's `avg_session_duration` ÷ 60, or the prior goal's value, or 0.
- **Confirmation:** the description tells the model to state the exact target and ask before calling it.

### Surfaces
- **Claude and ChatGPT:** all product behavior, through MCP tools, prompts and server instructions. `check_in` is the only on-track answer.
- **The web app:** `/login`, `/account`, `/connect` and OAuth consent only. `/connect` keeps telling people to start with the "Set up Therapay" prompt and drops the old "Set up my Therapay profile" line.
- **Frozen legacy:** `/dashboard`, `/planner`, `/profile` and `/api/chat` stay untouched and keep reading `sessions` (seed data). Removing them, along with the `sessions` table and the SQLite analytics replica, is a separate cleanup task, not part of this design.

**Effort:** M–L (human ~1–2 weeks / CC ~1 day). It covers:
- the migration
- `lib/pacing.ts`, `lib/goals.ts` and the rate-card and volume helpers
- 4 new tools, 3 tools retired, 3 MCP prompts, updated server instructions
- one copy change on `/connect` (the suggested first prompt)
- reconciliation switched to rate × volume
- a fixture test of the settled-cutoff, projection and lever math

## Eng Review (/plan-eng-review, 2026-11-03): amendments to the approved design

These decisions **supersede** the matching parts of "Recommended Approach" above.

### Corrected data model (D14, founder-confirmed)
- **Income and pacing use payouts only.** Stripe and platform payouts are the single source for YTD, pace, projection, goal gap and in-progress. Rate × volume is **never** added to income. The earlier "received if any, else expected" rule and the `basis` flag are removed.
- **The rate card** has one rate per **payer × CPT code** (`90837`, `90834`, `90791`, `90847`, `90853`), plus an optional `platform` and `effective_from` month. It's used only for mix analysis.
- **Monthly counts** per **payer × CPT code** per month: `completed`, `no_shows`, `late_cancels`, optional `late_cancel_fee`. Used only for mix analysis (blended rate, what-if swaps, sessions needed, no-show cost). No dates, no clients.
- **Levers:**
  - **Dollars per month needed:** from payouts.
  - **Sessions per month needed:** dollars needed ÷ her blended rate (rate card weighted by her last 3 months of counts, or by rates alone when there are no counts).
  - **Payer/CPT mix what-ifs:** from the rate card plus counts.
  - **No-show cost:** counts × rates.
  - **Weeks off she can afford:** from payouts.
- **`set_goal`** takes a dollar target or growth percentage, measured on received income. There's no `basis` parameter (D14 made it moot).
- **In-progress and next-payouts forecast (D12):** both come from her trailing payout history: the median of the last 4 payouts per paying entity, on that entity's usual payout weekday. In-progress is clamped at 0 and never counted toward YTD, pace or projection.
